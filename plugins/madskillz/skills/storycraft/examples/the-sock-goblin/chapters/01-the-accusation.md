# Chapter 1 — The Accusation

Mar had two striped socks on Monday morning.

She had zero striped socks by Tuesday.

"This," she told her notebook, "is a crime."

She had been keeping track. Seven socks gone in three weeks. Always just one from a pair. Never a plain white one. The sock thief had *taste*, which made it worse.

Mar uncapped her best pen and wrote: *Stakeout tonight. Laundry room. Bring flashlight.*

---

The laundry room smelled like warm fabric and dryer sheets. Mar crouched behind the laundry basket with her flashlight off and her notebook ready. The dryer rumbled and shuddered. The vent huffed a little puff of lint into the air, soft as a breath. Mar waited.

Nothing happened for a long time.

She ate a cracker. She wrote *nothing yet* in her notebook. She waited some more.

Then she heard it — a small, skittery sound. Like a mouse, but tidier.

Mar clicked on her flashlight.

A creature no bigger than a cereal box stood beside the dryer. It had round amber eyes, ears like folded paper, and very small hands. It was holding Mar's striped sock.

Mar's mouth dropped open.

The creature's eyes went wide. It clutched the sock to its chest like a baby bird.

"THIEF!" Mar yelled.

The creature let out a squeak and bolted — but Mar was faster. She grabbed the big metal colander from the dish rack and slammed it down over the creature like a dome.

The creature froze. Through the holes in the colander, two amber eyes stared up at her.

"Ha," said Mar. She sat on her heels and pointed her flashlight at the prisoner. "Got you. You're the sock goblin."

The creature straightened up very carefully. It smoothed its tiny jacket, which was stitched together from dryer-sheet squares. It cleared its throat.

"My name," it said, in a small but dignified voice, "is Pib."

"Okay, Pib." Mar flipped open her notebook. "Why did you steal my sock?"

Pib's ears went flat against his head. He looked at the sock in his hands, then at Mar. He took a breath so big that his whole chest puffed out.

"Technically," he said, "I was *saving* it."

Mar's pen stopped moving. She looked at the goblin. The striped sock was — now that she noticed — perfectly clean, neatly folded, and tucked against Pib's chest like something precious.

She looked back at Pib.

"Saving it," she repeated, "from *what?*"

Pib pressed his lips together. His amber eyes flickered toward a dark gap behind the dryer.

"That," he said quietly, "is a very long story."
